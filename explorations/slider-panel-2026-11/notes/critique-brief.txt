## The widget
A three-day weather strip (Today / Tomorrow / Saturday) in the plan panel. History: Round 3 restructured it around (a) three cloud-deck strata, (b) one shared sun-phase axis per card, (c) per-day status colour, (d) clay rain, (e) "window frames" — glowing tickets around shootable hours. Round 4 (current) refined it: frames wrap only blue+golden hours at dawn/dusk for the sun, and moonrise/moonset ±2h for the moon (gated: illumination ≥ 25% or NOTHING — a new moon is no moon; cover < 0.7, rain < 30%; bright vs soft by illumination). Rain per-hour drawn as type+intensity in four variants (fog-by-mm, bead census, fronts-vs-showers, cumulative wetness trace). ## The user (reconstruct them from evidence, then critique)
Everything in the app is shot-centric: it computes sunrise/sunset azimuths toward a 60–220km horizon and grades whether the sun will colour the sky; it plans tower-aligned shots at rise/set times; it shows a horizon compass strip with sun/moon bearings while framing at eye level. Winter in Berlin: direct sunlight is scarce; the app assumes the user wants sunlight/light events. The user plans hours-to-days ahead: "is tomorrow dawn worth getting up for? is Saturday's dusk a go?" They are NOT a meteorologist. Critically reflect: what decisions does this widget actually serve? What do they need to decide, in what order (go/no-go first? where to stand? what lens?)? What are they optimizing (a coloured sky? light on buildings? moon behind a landmark?). Where do the four Round-4 variants and the window logic serve that badly — e.g. is hour-binned 'good/fair/poor' the right granularity for events that live in 10-minute windows? Is the shared axis earning its place? Is the moonrise/moonset ±2h window honest given the moon's altitude changes slowly near the horizon? Is "clear midday earns no frame" right, given Berlin winter noon sun sits at ~17–18°?
