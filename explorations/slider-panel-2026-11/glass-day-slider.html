Three days of Berlin light in one slider

The track is the forecast, painted as sky. Every landmark stands at the minute it lines up, lit by the weather it will get. Drag the glass: it bends the scene and catches on each shot.

  • Time is stretched where photographers care. Dawn and dusk take most of each day's width; midday and deep night are squeezed.
  • Cloud is the sky itself. An overcast day is a flat grey stretch with dulled landmarks; a clear dusk glows. There is no separate cloud chart.
  • The sun and moon ride inside the lens at their height for that minute, so on a tower shot you see the disc slide behind the shaft.
  • Sketch data: three mock late-November days and mock alignment minutes for eight landmarks. Now is Thursday 13:20.
