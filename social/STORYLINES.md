# urbaneclipse social: storylines

Deliverables: one story (9:16, 1080×1920, about 25–30 s), one feed post (4:5, 1080×1350, the same film recomposed), and one short clip per feature (9:16, 6–10 s each, cut from the same footage and track).
Footage: recorded frame by frame from the real planner (Playwright), the same method as the landing clips. No mockups.
Music: generated with ElevenLabs Music (instrumental), about 100–115 bpm. Cuts land on beats measured with `hyperframes beats`.

## A. One day, one night (recommended)

- **Device and form:** the result first, then a flight through one world. The clock carries the film.
- **What you see:** the miniature Berlin runs from morning into deep night as a time-lapse. Each feature is a stop at its own time of day: the camera dives into the viewfinder, the shot plays, and then the camera pulls back out into the city.
- **First two seconds:** the TV tower sphere in a 200 mm frame, with the sun disc half hidden behind it. "12:37:15" ticks in the corner. Title: "The sun behind the TV tower".
- **Beats:**
  1. Hook: the eclipse in the viewfinder.
  2. Pull back to the city at noon. Title: "Berlin, planned to the second".
  3. Time-lapse into evening, then a street henge: the sun sets down the street.
  4. The night drop: the city goes dark and its lights come on.
  5. The moon on the antenna.
  6. Drone: rise to 110 m.
  7. "Check dates": the year as a list.
  8. Claim: "Find your shot."
  9. End card: urbaneclipse.oratio.co
- **Look:** the planner's own night palette. The viewfinder frame is the one recurring shape. White type, condensed and large. One accent colour (the sun's orange) for the payoff and the end card.
- **Sound:**
  - Calm, pulsing electronica that is held back during the day.
  - At the cut into the night the drums drop in. The moon lands on the strongest beat.
  - No effects except a soft shutter click at each viewfinder snap.

## B. When, where, which lens

- **Device and form:** type carries the argument, with hard cuts on the beat.
- **What you see:** big questions in kinetic type ("When?", "Where to stand?", "Which lens?"). The planner answers each one with a real clip, cut on every beat, so it reads like a manifesto for photographers.
- **First two seconds:** black-and-white type "Manhattanhenge?" cuts hard to the sun straight down a Berlin street. Then "Berlinhenge."
- **Beats:**
  1. Question.
  2. Answer clip.
  3. Repeat steps 1 and 2 three times (henge, tower eclipse, moon).
  4. "All of it, for every day of the year."
  5. Fast montage of features: viewfinder, drone, dates, calendar.
  6. End card.
- **Look:** colour fields that alternate between night blue and sun orange, very large type, and planner clips full-frame between them.
- **Sound:** a driving 120 bpm with a cut on every half beat. Fast and loud, made for the feed.

## C. Out of the photo (the bold one)

- **Device and form:** an endless zoom backwards. The camera never cuts.
- **What you see:**
  1. It starts like a finished photo of the moon on the antenna.
  2. The camera pulls back: the photo is the viewfinder, the viewfinder sits at eye level on a street, then it rises as a drone, out to the whole night city.
  3. The time-lapse runs backwards into the day, and the camera dives into the next shot.
- **First two seconds:** a quiet, almost still "photo": the full moon on the antenna, with no UI. Then a slight pull back reveals the frame corners.
- **Beats:**
  1. Photo.
  2. Pull back to the viewfinder.
  3. Pull back to the street.
  4. Pull back to the drone.
  5. Pull back to the city.
  6. Time-lapse.
  7. Dive into the sun eclipse.
  8. Claim.
  9. End card.
- **Look:** a single camera move with no hard cuts. Night dominates.
- **Sound:** ambient pads that slowly build, with no drum cuts (it is the least "music-based cut" option).
