## Plan 1: find the landmarks people actually photograph with the sun or moon

Goal: model the landmarks people actually photograph with the sun or moon, ranked by how often.

1. **Research.** Look at what people actually shoot, sorted by how often the motif appears:
   - Flickr API (tags/text: `moon` or `sunset` together with `berlin` and the landmark name, geotagged);
   - Instagram and 500px searches by hand;
   - PhotoPills and TPE community spots;
   - articles such as "best moonrise spots Berlin".

   Expected top list, to be checked:
   - Fernsehturm (done)
   - Brandenburger Tor
   - Siegessäule (Goldelse)
   - Reichstag dome
   - Berliner Dom
   - Oberbaumbrücke (done)
   - Molecule Man
   - Funkturm
   - Rotes Rathaus
   - Marienkirche
   - Frankfurter Tor (done)
   - Kaiser-Wilhelm-Gedächtniskirche
   - Victory column view from Straße des 17. Juni
   - Park Inn / Alexa
   - Treptowers
   - Gasometer Schöneberg

   Write the result to `docs/photo-landmarks.md`: rank, why people shoot it, typical camera spots, and LoD2 state.
2. **LoD2 check** for each landmark: what the tiles have. The scratchpad probe scripts did this by decoding `public/city/<tile>-0.bin` with meshoptimizer and listing vertex heights inside the OSM footprint. A small script in `scripts/miniature/` would make that reusable.
   - The Siegessäule is a 67 m column; LoD2 almost certainly has only a stub.
   - The Brandenburger Tor's columns and quadriga are missing.
   - Siegessäule and Funkturm need to be in the area (|x|, |z| < 9400 m around the Fernsehturm). The Funkturm is about 11 km west, so it is probably outside.
3. **Model** them in `landmarks.js`, following the pattern there:
   - Take positions and footprints from OSM building parts (Overpass query: `way["building:part"](bbox)` and `man_made`). Write down where the heights come from.
   - Hide the LoD2 roof with `uCuts` in `shading.js`. Raise `CUTS` per landmark; it is a square cut above a height.
   - Thin landmarks (a column, a mast) can be lathe profiles like the Fernsehturm in `render.js` / `src/lib/landmarks.ts`.
4. **Eclipse maths.** The tower-shot rules (`towerShots.js`, `dates.worker.js`, `src/lib/landmarks.ts`) only know the Fernsehturm. A second target, for example the Siegessäule with the moon, would mean generalising "sun or moon behind the tower" to a list of targets. That is a bigger step: plan it separately, with tests in `tests/unit/miniature.test.ts`.

Still open from the trees work:

- Woods inside parks are missing.

## Plan 2: composite images for the homepage (Alibaba / Bailian)

Idea: use the planner's real dates, places and angles (landmark + sun or moon, the right size for the lens) as a base. Then generate photo-like composites with Alibaba (skill `bailian-gen`, `bl image`), image-to-image from a planner still or text with exact framing.

1. **Base.** For each top landmark, take one real upcoming date, spot and lens from the planner: share link plus still, via `scripts/miniature/record_clips.mjs` or a screenshot. Fix the sun or moon size by the lens, from `lensScale.ts`.
2. **Generate.** Image-to-image from the still: the planner fixes composition and position, and the model adds photographic surface. Keep the sun or moon size and phase from the planner, not what the model invents. Check every image against the planner view (position, size, phase, lighting direction). Discard any that drift.
3. **Labelling: please decide this deliberately.** Tobias wants them shown "like real photos". Recommendation: show them in a photo look, but labelled visibly as a visualisation (for example "Visualisierung · 22 Mar 2027, 18:04, Gitschiner Str."), and with a link into the planner for that moment.

   Reasons:
   - Generated images presented unlabelled as real photos mislead visitors.
   - Photographers notice it, and on a site that sells real moments, that costs trust.
   - The EU AI Act requires synthetic images to be marked as generated.

   Once real photos exist (Tobias's own, `treppmannphotography`), swap them in.
4. **Where.** The homepage (`src/pages/index.astro`) currently uses planner clips from `public/media`. The composites would go into an example strip, each image with its date, place and "Open in planner".
