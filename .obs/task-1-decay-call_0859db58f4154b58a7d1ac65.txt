5:86:In 1538, a new western façade with two towers was attached to the collegiate church, which – due to its prior status as a church of a [[Mendicant orders|mendicant order]] – had no tower before. In the next year, Joachim II Hector converted from Catholicism to [[Lutheranism]], as many of his subjects had done earlier. The ''collegiate church'' thus became Lutheran too, like most of the electoral subjects and all the churches in the Electorate. However, Joachim II Hector's ideas of [[Protestant Reformation|Reformation]] were different from the modern ones. After his conversion he enriched the collegiate church with luxuriant furnishings, such as [[parament]]s, [[monstrance]]s, [[relic]]s, [[chasuble]]s, carpets and [[Antependium|antependia]].<ref>{{harvnb|Gottschalk|1985|p=169}}</ref> From 1545 on the electoral family of [[Hohenzollern]] used the church building as their burial place.<ref>{{harvnb|Kühne|Stephani|1986|p=361}}</ref>
6:88:In 1608, the year of his accession to the throne, Prince-Elector [[John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg|John Sigismund]], then a [[Crypto-Calvinism|crypto-Calvinist]], dissolved the [[College (canon law)|college]] and the church was renamed into '''Supreme Parish Church of Holy Trinity in Cölln'''.<ref name="Elisabeth Stephani 1986, p. 362">{{harvnb|Kühne|Stephani|1986|p=362}}</ref> In 1613, John Sigismund publicly confessed his [[Calvinist]] faith (in Germany usually called [[Continental Reformed church|Reformed Church]]), but waived his privilege to demand the same of his subjects ([[Cuius regio, eius religio]]). So he and his family, except his steadfastly Lutheran wife [[Duchess Anna of Prussia|Anna]], converted, while most of his subjects remained Lutherans. While Berlin's other churches, subject to Lutheran city-council jurisdiction, remained Lutheran, the ''Supreme Parish Church of Holy Trinity'', the Hohenzollern's house church, became Berlin's first, and until 1695, only Calvinist church,<ref>In 1695 Berlin's second Calvinist church was erected, called [[Parochialkirche|Parochial Church]], i.e. parish church, as opposed to the Supreme Parish Church then colloquially called Collegiate of Palace Church.</ref> serving from 1632 on as the parish for all Calvinists in town.<ref>From then on Calvinist immigrants, as from [[Bohemia]], France ([[Huguenots]]), [[United Duchies of Jülich-Cleves-Berg|Juliers-Cleves-Berg]], the [[Netherlands]], Poland, [[Switzerland]], and [[Wallonia]] were very welcome in Berlin and all the Electorate of Brandenburg in order to build up a considerable minority, being religiously a power base of the Hohenzollern.</ref> Being now a Calvinist church, the patronage of the [[Holy Trinity]] was increasingly skipped.
